    1 · The probe

    An Agentic Probe is an instrument for discovery, not a draft

    DEFINITION · PAPER §3A stakeholder-runnable instance of a candidate solution to a stated pain. Throwaway-grade by design: its job is to validate or falsify the pain, not to be production-ready.Pointed at the problem instead, the same probe renders today’s process or a possible future.

    Agentic

    An AI agent builds it from the stakeholder’s own words, in minutes.

    Usable

    The stakeholder uses it as if it were real, instead of reading about it.

    Disposable

    It answers a question about the problem, then it is thrown away.

    5 · The probe

    A probe asks one of two questions: is this the pain? Would this solve it?

    6 · Agentic Discovery

    Agentic Discovery is the expedition; the probe is its instrument

    Agentic Discovery optimises for learning speed. It explores the problem with probes; specs are derived from what the probes found. Minutes to hours, in the outer layer.
    Agentic Engineering optimises for production reliability. The probe’s spec is its input; its output is production-grade code. Days to sprints, in the middle and core layers.

    7 · Agentic Discovery

    Three roles turn a sentence into a probe, through two safety gates

    Dreamer

    A domain expert who can describe the need in plain language. Writes no code.

    Orchestrator

    Turns ideas into tractable prompts, checks safety, protects the agent queue.

    AI agent

    A coding agent (Claude Code) reads the queue, builds the Probe, deploys and reports.

    Zero safety incidents were logged across all probe generations: two independent checks before any code is written.
    Without a named Orchestrator, sessions either stalled on safety questions or drifted into free exploration.

    8 · Agentic Discovery

    Probes live in the outer layer and never touch the legacy core

    Outer layer: Agentic Discovery

    New processes are tried here, and probes are created at industrial speed.

    Middle layer: integration

    APIs, connectors and modernisation bridges. Validated probes move inward only through deliberate, reviewed steps.

    Core: legacy IT

    The stable foundation. The outer layers extend it; they do not replace it.

    FIELD OBSERVATIONNo field site let a discovery session reach the legacy core. The layered model was the precondition for being let into the building at all.

    12 · The finding

    When probes are cheap, the bottleneck moves: production, orchestration, ideas

    EVENTS 1–4 · SMALL OFFICE GROUPS

    The agent keeps up

    2–4 parallel requests

    Every requested probe was built and shown back live, in the room.

    No bottleneck
    EVENT 5 · HIGHER CONCURRENCY

    One person holds the queue

    7 parallel requests

    Queue management, prompt hygiene and session tracking all fell back onto one person.

    Limit: the human Orchestrator
    TWO LARGEST · ≈100 INVITED, HYBRID

    Every idea gets built

    orchestration partly automated

    A small tool batched briefs and dispatched agents in parallel. Production stopped limiting.

    Limit: idea supply & validation speed
    Every layer you automate exposes the next one. The rate limit is now the queue at the inlet, not the throughput.
    Same shift in the problem-validating mode: the limit is how fast people author the next “what if”12 / 17
    13 · The probe as simulation

    A probe can be today's process, simulated, then relaxed one constraint at a time

    Case 1 · Renewing a chronic prescription

    visit + lab + e-prescription + pharmacy→AI-assisted visit→CE-marked agent renews, routes to a public lab

    Case 2 · A sick child on the eve of a trip

    day-long public path, “no travel”→same-day private visit→AI-assisted self-care, evidence-based advice

    The probe is the relaxation

    The first scenario re-enacts how the process runs today. The stakeholder can stop it, correct it, or fork it. There is no software handoff across the seam.

    Case 3 · A skin symptom on a Friday evening

    Honest scope: a researcher-authored companion, n = 3 cases in one domain, not a field study. Costs are the companion's simulated estimates per path.

    16 · What we do not claim

    Six limits on these claims, and what replication should test

    L1

    Small, self-selected sample

    Eight engagements, two voluntary organisations. May not transfer where people cannot describe pains in conversation.

    → more organisations, regulated domains
    L2

    Throwaway-grade code

    Probes validate fit, not production quality. The multiplier must not be projected onto delivery.

    → measure the residual multiplier
    L3

    One agent vendor

    Every probe came from Claude Code. Patterns may or may not be vendor-independent.

    → Codex, Cursor, Copilot, open-weight agents
    L4

    Effort baseline

    50 lines per day varies by domain and maturity. It moves the number, not the qualitative finding.

    → alternative baselines
    L5

    Researchers as Orchestrators

    The authors often orchestrated. An independent or less skilled one might get different results.

    → non-author orchestrators
    L6

    Problem-validating only as a companion

    Field scale covers the solution-proposing mode. The healthcare cases are illustrative, n = 3.

    → field-scale process discovery

    17 · What follows

    Design for discovery: production is cheap, so the inlet decides

    Name an Orchestrator

    The role that turns sentences into probes. It keeps prompts tractable and the agent queue protected, and it is the first thing to automate as you scale.

    Open an outer layer

    Give probes a safe place to run, away from the legacy core, and a reviewed path for the ones that hold up to move inward.

    Measure the inlet

    Count questions asked and probes judged, not lines of code. Idea supply and validation speed are where the next constraint lives.
